Introduction

The objective of this report is to outline how the data from the American Time Use Survey (ATUS) can help us design and launch ‘It’s About Time’, an app to help users compare how they use their time against people in the same demographic.

ATUS is an American federal survey conducted by the Bureau of Labor Statistics and U.S. Census Bureau to measure how Americans spend their time.

Data Highlights

Since 2003, 252,808 people have responded to the ATUS survey.

ATUS tracks 77 unique sports and recreation related activities, including watching sports. Respondents reported watching 32 different sports, making it a popular activity across diverse interests.

The current full retirement age for US Social Security is 67 years old, with 15.4% of respondents falling into this category. However, approximately 16.4% of Americans reported being retired meaning.

Experts recommend that adults sleep between 7 and 9 hours per night. On average, Americans reported sleeping 8.8 hours per night.

On average, Americans with one or more child in the home reported spending 2 hours taking care of their children.

Further Data Exploration

  • Q1: Household Activities by Age
  • Q2: Education Workers in Summer
  • Q3: Education Time by Student Age
  • Q4: Sports Interest Bump Plot
  • Q5: Childcare Time by Family Size

As Americans age, their time spent on household activities steadily increases, peaking for those in the 66-75 age group at 3 hours per day. This aligns with about the first decade of retirement, where people are likely to have a lot of free time to attend to household activities. The average time then decreases in the 76+ range, a time in life when people require more physical assistance and may not be capable of doing all household activities on their own, though the decrease is only a few minutes less per day.

During the summer break, Americans who work in education primarily spend their time doing activities in the Relaxing and Leisure for 19.2% of their time, followed by Working for 17.2% of their time. This shows that while education workers aren’t spending the majority of time working during the summer months, some are still working despite schools not being in session.

Among college students, those aged 15-22, 23-30 dedicate the most time to educational activities at 5, 5 hours per day, while the 41-50 age group spends the least at 4.4 hours. Traditional-age students (15-22) average 5 hours per day on education.

Among younger Americans (15-25), Attending movies or film ranks as the most watched, while Attending gambling establishments is the most popular among those 66 and older. Attending museums sees the biggest rise in popularity with age, while Watching football declines most in relative interest.

As family size increases, total childcare time rises modestly but time per child decreases substantially. Parents with one child spend 1.8 hours per day on childcare, all dedicated to that single child. Parents with two children spend 2 hours total, about 1 hours per child, a 44.4% drop in individual attention. By four children, parents spend 2.2 hours total but only 0.6 hours per child. This pattern supports the “middle-child syndrome” hypothesis that each additional child dilutes the amount of individual parental attention.

Time Use Patterns in Target Demographics

  • Stay-at-Home Dads
  • College Undergraduates
  • Employed, Single Women (30-35)

Target Demographic: Stay-at-home dads — married men who are not in the labor force and have at least one child under 18 in the home. There are 2,039 respondents matching this profile in the ATUS data.

Key Activities: Childcare, cooking, housework, sleeping, TV & leisure, and exercise. These represent the core daily activities where stay-at-home dads can track and improve their time allocation — particularly maximizing childcare and exercise while managing household duties.
